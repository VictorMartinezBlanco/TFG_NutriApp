\chapter*{Abstract}

\section*{\tituloPortadaEngVal}

This Bachelor's Thesis presents the design of NutriApp, a web platform intended to centralise the workflow of professional nutritionists. The idea came from observing closely how a nutritionist works day to day: his information was scattered across physical notebooks, Google Forms, Google Calendar, his clinic's platform, phone notes and WhatsApp. To analyse the complete situation of a single client, data from all these tools had to be cross-referenced by hand, which is inefficient and makes it likely that information gets lost along the way.

Starting from this real problem, the initial proposal was to design a web application that would bring all these functions together in a single point of access. The platform would be structured in two separate panels: one for the nutritionist, from which they can manage clients, appointments, nutritional plans and the messaging with their clients; and one for the client, with its own interface to consult their plan, record completed meals and weight, communicate with their nutritionist and follow their progress.

When that first idea was contrasted with more practising nutritionists, the project finally moved towards its most innovative feature: the creation of personalised diets for each client, which became the differentiating element of the tool. Such a specific tool can only be carried out successfully when a large part of the information about the user is connected, making it possible to access their allergens, intolerances, nutritional goals, preferences and habits, among others. At the same time, the rules, preferences and restrictions that the nutritionist imposes on the programme are also highly relevant, since they condition the resulting plan. In the final version, the nutritionist asks for what they need in their own language; a language model, run locally so that clinical data never leave the system, translates that request into formal constraints; an optimisation engine builds the plan over the foods stored and modelled in the database (each one with its allergen and food family tags, and with its macronutrients and properties) and an independent validator audits it; and the plan only reaches the client once the professional has reviewed and signed it. The result is a platform with two roles, nutritionist and client, whose core is that copilot, and its potential lies in relieving the professional of the task that takes them the most time.
